What wb-templates is

wb-templates is the reusable scientific-computing layer of Agentic Workbench (wb). It brings together code, data readers, plotting styles and document examples that a researcher can copy into a new project. The aim is to make it possible to reuse an old calculation or a familiar figure routine while keeping the assumptions, inputs and checks needed to understand the result.

Park describes this problem in his Agentic Workbench project letter. His research codes were tested for particular questions, and he reused his own Python analysis and visualization routines over many years. When a new question comes up, the useful parts can outlast the context in which they were written. The templates show how to recover that context, retain an original comparison and adapt the parts that the new project needs. The receiving researcher chooses the scientific model, applicability domain and criteria for using the result.

Within wb, wb-plugins supplies reusable procedures for people and agents. wb-templates supplies examples and reusable source. wb-applications can assemble selected parts into an environment for a particular task. The templates can also be copied and used independently with their declared dependencies.

Scientific Composability for Multiscale Simulation

A particle simulation and a continuum calculation can share a physical quantity if both sides agree on its definition, units, applicable conditions and numerical handling. For example, a continuum filtration solver can query a table of concentration-dependent transport properties derived from a particle-scale calculation. The solver needs to know what each ratio means, which concentrations have physical support and how the table handles queries between or beyond its recorded points. A composition check observes the two components exchanging and using the quantities under stated conditions.

The collection documents three levels of modernization. Choose the level that matches the change you need and the evidence you can collect.

Level Work and evidence
1. Preserve a baseline Retain a runnable calculation, record its inputs and interfaces, and pin outputs for comparison.
2. Modernize the implementation Preserve the numerical architecture and check changed operations and selected results against retained references.
3. Compose scientific components Work toward scientific composability for multiscale simulation. Define the exchanged quantity and run a producer–consumer check under specified physical and numerical conditions.

The BD–MC modernization guide and mBLA modernization overview describe the evidence available at each level for their respective cases.

A current Level 3 example

An internal wb-applications project couples the native BD–MC particle model to the mBLA continuum solver. The live path calls BD–MC to evaluate transport properties for the filtration calculation. The tested live evaluations are computationally expensive, so the practical route uses interpolation of an offline table of calculated properties over its stated range. The application is internal. Development is extending live evaluation to conditions where the solver needs a fresh property calculation. The same interface could support a future ML surrogate after its training data, validity range and predictions are independently validated.

Worked use cases

Inspect the embedded cylinder regression

Open the mBLA profile explorer in a browser. It starts offline with the embedded cylinder regression at CASE2 (b = 1, nominal transmembrane pressure (TMP) 1.15 kPa). In Axial profile, choose a quantity such as wall concentration and move the Stored axial station slider through the 200 stored stations. Beside it, Outlet radial profile has its own Stored radial sample slider for the 400 samples at z/L = 1. The radial readout gives wall distance y/R, radius from the axis r/R, and y/δ_CP for the case’s fixed concentration-polarization length. The tables give the archive and replay values and signed differences.

Open Site hollow-fibre reference to investigate the saved Python suspension at nominal TMP 5 kPa. Choose a point on the shaded cutaway to see concentration and the selected velocity component along both directions. This site preview uses its own fixed CP length and a separate dataset from the JCP archive comparison and the internal coupling benchmark.

To inspect a compatible file, select Open profile data. The data guide explains the formats. The explorer displays saved results locally in the browser.

Update a solver or exchange a quantity

Start from one traceable calculation you trust. Save the original inputs, recorded outputs and tolerances. In BD–MC, the portable legacy executable is checked with frozen regressions, and the modern components have checks for selected topology, transition and clock operations. The separate equilibrium EOS example exports observations from a native hard-sphere sampler. A Python process reads them and estimates pressure. The run covers equilibrium pressure and near-contact structure under the stated model conditions. For your own exchange, define the physical quantity and check the supplier and receiving calculation together.

For a small comparison with retained results, open the BD–MC equilibrium EOS example. It compares soft-repulsive Brownian dynamics and hard-sphere Monte Carlo with the Carnahan–Starling equation. The example includes the measured CSV, both figure formats and the scope of the finite-size checks.

Read measurements, make figures and share the comparison

Inspect a CSV header and select the numeric columns explicitly with Scientific CSV I/O. Choose units, transformations and axis limits according to the measurements. Apply plot styles to use consistent axes and switch between color and monochrome. Use wb-style when you want the separate preset derived from Park’s original plotting scripts. The collection plotting example generates its own synthetic CSV and writes PNG, PDF and rendering metadata.

To communicate the result, copy the Quarto comparison example. Use its report to explain quantities, cases and acceptance criteria in full, and its slides to show the selected figure and link back to the report. Replace the synthetic data and source comparison with the inputs from your own study.

Reproduce and adapt

  1. Choose the scope. Decide what you want to reproduce or extend. Read the selected template’s requirements, test procedure and evidence limits.
  2. Make an independent copy. Keep the source, small fixtures, tests, license, notices and provenance together. The copying contract and collection commands describe the local exporter and manifest check.
  3. Run the supplied example in the stated environment. Use a fresh output directory. Record the source edition, inputs, command and result so that you can rerun the same case later.
  4. Change one part at a time. Keep your original comparison. For a solver, record changes to assumptions, boundary conditions, units, numerical methods or estimators. For a figure or document, keep the input data distinct from style and layout settings.
  5. Check what you will rely on. Compare the adapted result with a relevant reference or invariant under your own conditions. For an exchanged quantity, check that the receiving component interprets the values and applicable range as intended.

Evidence and limits

  • BD–MC. The modernization record reports nine frozen current-port regressions, including driven shear cases with changes in network topology. The detached Level 3 check covers a native sampler and independent pressure analysis. Full modern active-shear correspondence, scientific validation of RPY and Stokesian dynamics, and broader post-processing validation remain open. The equilibrium MC sweep counts attempted moves.
  • mBLA. The modernization overview records eight selected cases on the 200 × 400 grid. Level 2 matched Level 1 files byte-for-byte and the archived mean pressures and permeate velocities at stored precision. A separate non-cake 5 kPa, 10 nm case checked live and pretabulated transport providers. The evidence applies to the specified cases, quantities and models. Broader operating conditions and mesh convergence need their own checks.
  • wb-python. The validation reference gives the tested runtime editions and the scope of the request and Profile checks.
  • Plotting, I/O and publishing. The supplied examples use synthetic data to exercise plotting, numeric selection and publishing. Their evidence covers those operations. Original research figures and physical results have separate sources and acceptance criteria.

Use the collection license scope and the selected template’s notices when copying code, data or rendered assets.
