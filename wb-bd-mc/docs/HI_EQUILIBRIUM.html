Equilibrium Brownian transport with free-space pair HI

What the benchmark compares

This two-sphere test uses the same temperature, solvent, separation interval, potential and initial conditions for three thermal dynamics: pure BD with unit normalized mobility (Ksed=1K_\mathrm{sed}=1), RPY BD, and Brownian evolution based on the deterministic Stokesian mobility with lubrication. All use an unbounded solvent and a reflecting constraint on the pair separation. Fluid mobility is evaluated in free space. The pair configuration is bounded radially and free to rotate.

The Level 3 template provides the repulsive pair force, RPY mobility and the deterministic Stokesian solver. The example builds a separate thermal pair integrator from those mobilities. It uses neither the default scalar BD integrator nor the full-coordinate native HI stepper for this constrained radial experiment. It leaves both standalone solvers available for their own problems.

Use reduced units σ=kBT=D0=1\sigma=k_BT=D_0=1, radius a=σ/2a=\sigma/2, ηs*=1/(3π)\eta_s^*=1/(3\pi) and τσ=σ2/D0\tau_\sigma=\sigma^2/D_0. The two spheres have 1.05≤r/σ≤2.501.05\le r/\sigma\le2.50. The template polynomial repulsion uses ϵ/kBT=24\epsilon/k_BT=24 and cutoff 1.65σ1.65\sigma. The reflecting shell retains a positive gap compatible with the SD resistance table. Its volume sets the normalization for reported pair stress. It is a separation-space volume and carries no bulk-volume-fraction interpretation.

Translational mobility and thermal dynamics

C++ grid producer calls bdmc::hydrodynamic_mobility and bdmc::solve_stokesian. In the SD solver, opposite unit forces yield the relative diffusivities D∥D_\parallel and D⟂D_\perp in reduced units. Equal forces yield the parallel and transverse center diffusivities Dcm,∥D_{\mathrm{cm},\parallel} and Dcm,⟂D_{\mathrm{cm},\perp} after dividing the center velocity by the total force. Lubrication is enabled. The pure BD coefficients are 2 for relative motion and 1/21/2 for the center, in all directions.

Thermal pair runner uses the equilibrium density p(r)∝w(r)=r2e−U(r)/kBTp(r)\propto w(r)=r^2e^{-U(r)/k_BT}. Its radial backward generator is Lf=w−1∂r(wD∥∂rf). Lf=w^{-1}\partial_r(wD_\parallel\,\partial_r f). The expansion gives D∥f″+[D′∥+2D∥/r−D∥U′/kBT]f′D_\parallel f''+ [D'_\parallel+2D_\parallel/r-D_\parallel U'/k_BT]f'. The radial diffusion-gradient and geometric drifts are included through this divergence form. Adjacent cell rates share the conductance wfaceD∥,face/h2w_\mathrm{face}D_{\parallel,\mathrm{face}}/h^2; divide by the source center weight. Boundary faces carry no flux. Detailed balance follows from equal forward and reverse flux at the Boltzmann weights. The angular generator is D⟂r−2ΔΩD_\perp r^{-2}\Delta_\Omega. The resulting thermal process is the reduced two-sphere dynamics of the chosen mobility and potential. The deterministic SD library supplies a response matrix and stresslets. The example supplies thermal evolution separately.

Diffusivity, viscosity and pressure

Diffusivity

The measured equivalent of unit normalized mobility in this test is Hpair=2⟨(Dcm,∥+2Dcm,⟂)/3⟩/D0H_\mathrm{pair}=2\langle (D_{\mathrm{cm},\parallel}+2D_{\mathrm{cm},\perp})/3\rangle/D_0. The factor of two normalizes to the free-draining pair reference. The average is over the common isotropic equilibrium ensemble. Pair mobility is a center response, whereas the relative coefficient in LL sets internal relaxation. Neither coefficient is injected as a bulk mBLA Dc/D0D_c/D_0. The explicit HI cases apply no empirical KsedK_\mathrm{sed} multiplier.

Viscosity

Define q(r)=−rU′(r)q(r)=-r U'(r) and the smooth pair-pressure deviator Pd=(q/Vshell)(𝐧𝐧−I/3)P^d=(q/V_\mathrm{shell})(\mathbf n\mathbf n-I/3). The Green–Kubo stress contraction in isotropic equilibrium samples the second angular harmonic. Conditional on the radial path, its correlation factor is exp[−∫0t6D⟂(rs)/rs2ds]\exp[-\int_0^t6 D_\perp(r_s)/r_s^2\,ds]. The tensor contraction contributes 2/32/3, so the configurational increment in simulation units is Δηconf(tc)ηs=3π15Vshell*∫0tc⟨q*(r0)q*(rt)exp[−∫0t6D⟂(rs)/rs2ds]⟩dt. \frac{\Delta\eta_\mathrm{conf}(t_c)}{\eta_s} =\frac{3\pi}{15\,V_\mathrm{shell}^*} \int_0^{t_c} \left\langle q^*(r_0)q^*(r_t) \exp[-\int_0^t6D_\perp(r_s)/r_s^2\,ds] \right\rangle\,dt . The spectral contraction evaluates the radial generator minus the angular decay rate. The simulator independently samples the radial transitions and evaluates the conditional factor along the paths. The reported increment refers to the smooth configurational force; the table lists both the finite 0.4τσ0.4\tau_\sigma window and the spectral infinite-lag limit of this bounded model.

The SD solver also returns an instantaneous stresslet response. A symmetric traceless five-mode strain basis gives the isotropic trace of the stresslet operator. Divide by 2ηsVshell2\eta_s V_\mathrm{shell} to obtain a relative high-frequency increment. It is a separate diagnostic. The RPY translational mobility provides no stresslet operator. This experiment does not supply Brownian stresslet and stress cross terms or a total suspension viscosity.

Osmotic pressure and equilibrium

The common smooth-force virial contribution is ⟨q⟩/(3Vshell)\langle q\rangle/(3 V_\mathrm{shell}). The relative shell's outer thermodynamic load is kBTe−U(rmax)/kBT/[4π∫rminrmaxr2e−U(r)/kBTdr]k_BT e^{-U(r_\max)/k_BT}/[4\pi\int_{r_\min}^{r_\max}r^2e^{-U(r)/k_BT}dr]. It is the derivative of the relative configurational partition function when increasing its outer volume at fixed inner radius. It is not the periodic osmotic equation of state. A bulk periodic calculation would instead evaluate the ideal particle term and all applicable interaction virial terms in the actual box at fixed ϕ\phi.

Equal weights and detailed balance predict the same equilibrium separation law and static pressure under the three mobilities. Different relative diffusivities and angular relaxation rates can produce different time correlations on that same ensemble.

The matched pure/RPY/SD comparison is valid for the same isolated-pair geometry, equilibrium weights and smooth-force definitions. Reproduce the benchmark

Requires C++17, CMake and GSL for the native grid, and Python with NumPy and SciPy for the thermal calculation. Run from the template directory.

work="$(mktemp -d)"
cmake -S modern -B "$work/build" -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release \
  -DBDMC_BUILD_PYTHON=OFF -DBDMC_BUILD_BENCHMARKS=ON -DBDMC_BUILD_HYDRODYNAMICS=ON
cmake --build "$work/build" --parallel 2 --target bdmc_hi_equilibrium_grid
mkdir "$work/grids"
for cells in 72 144 288; do
  "$work/build/bdmc_hi_equilibrium_grid" "$cells" > "$work/grids/mobility-$cells.csv"
done
python3 tools/equilibrium_hi_pair.py --grids "$work/grids" --output "$work/results"

Output tables record mobility/transport, sampled equilibrium endpoints from two different starts, the running Green–Kubo integral, and sampled finite integrals with Monte Carlo standard error. Floating values use 17 significant digits in scientific notation. A grid refinement, analytical RPY pair, isolated SD stresslet, far-separation drag and paired thermal-equilibrium checks are included in this reference workflow. The 144-cell stress test uses 4,096 paths for each of two seeds.

Fixed-density finite-size test

The pair result supplies a check of the thermal model. To test size sensitivity, a second example samples equilibrium configurations in a cube with reflecting center boundaries. It matches ϕ=0.10\phi=0.10, N=8,16,32,64N=8,16,32,64 and L/σ=(Nπ/(6ϕ))1/3L/\sigma=(N\pi/(6\phi))^{1/3}. The pair energy and minimum separation are the same as above. Each doubling in NN scales the side by 21/32^{1/3}. The mechanical cube admits particle centers. The mobility solver still uses unbounded solvent, without periodic images or fluid walls.

C++ finite-size benchmark equilibrates each size with single-particle Metropolis moves. Use two seeds, discard 2,000 sweeps, and then record six blocks of 160 sweeps per seed. Compute mobility and SD stresslets on the same last configuration of each block. The Markov chain samples equilibrium. Its sweep number does not set a Brownian time. This design compares the equal-time mobility and the instantaneous SD stresslet. Only the separate pair experiment measures a thermal stress correlation.

Apply equal force to each sphere in three Cartesian directions and compute H0open=13Nμ0⟨∑ijtrMij⟩. H_0^\mathrm{open} =\frac{1}{3N\mu_0}\left\langle\sum_{ij}\operatorname{tr}M_{ij}\right\rangle . The free-draining identity gives one. Evaluate the SD stresslet in a symmetric xyxy affine strain and normalize by 2ηsVE:E2\eta_s V E:E. The reported SD quantity represents this resolved stresslet contribution. The benchmark provides no equivalent RPY stresslet.

For pressure sample each fourth sweep of every production block. Use virtual compression V′=Ve−ϵV'=Ve^{-\epsilon} with ϵ=0.002,0.004\epsilon=0.002,0.004. The compression estimator is Π*=Nϵ−ln⟨e−ΔU/kBT𝟏allowed⟩(V−V′)/σ3. \Pi^* = \frac{N\epsilon-\ln\langle e^{-\Delta U/k_BT} \mathbf 1_\mathrm{allowed}\rangle}{(V-V')/\sigma^3}. Particle-center distances scale by e−ϵ/3e^{-\epsilon/3}; a compressed configuration with a disallowed gap contributes zero weight. The NϵN\epsilon term comes from the coordinate Jacobian. Use the smaller compression step for the primary number and retain both to show finite perturbation sensitivity. This is the mechanical confinement pressure of the chosen cube. It is shared by all three mobility models on the same samples.

The size trend in open-fluid mobility is consistent with summing the long-range 1/r1/r coupling under a net common force. It does not converge to bulk KsedK_\mathrm{sed} in this test. The SD instantaneous stresslet varies much less over the tested sizes. The pressure changes with the finite cube size, even on the shared equilibrium ensemble. Two seeds and one density bound the interpretation. Compact plots can show the seed-to-seed range and both compression steps, without treating them as a confidence interval or extrapolating thermodynamic limits.
