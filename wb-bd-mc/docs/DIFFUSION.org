#+title: Self and collective diffusion in equilibrium BD–MC
#+date: 2026-11-25
#+language: en

The estimators below use tagged positions and coherent density sampled on a
declared observation clock. The reference model is equilibrium free-draining
Brownian dynamics. Scientific validation of the full transport-analysis
workflow remains pending.

* Primary reference for colloidal collective diffusion
A. J. Banchio, M. Heinen, P. Holmqvist and G. Nägele,
“Short- and long-time diffusion and dynamic scaling in suspensions of
charged colloidal particles,” /J. Chem. Phys./ *148*, 134902 (2018).
This is the scientific reference for relating colloidal structure, density
relaxation, and short- and long-time diffusion. The benchmark here uses
a neutral finite-range stiff cosine interaction without HI. Its values
are not a reproduction of the reference's charge-stabilized model.

The exact citation is listed as reference 69 in Nägele et al.,
/Theory of Colloidal Suspension Structure, Dynamics, and Rheology/
(2021). The notation below follows the directly reviewed 2004 notes.

* Dynamic structure factor and collective diffusion and relation to mBLA
Use \(S_s(q,t)\) for the self-dynamic structure factor and \(S_c(q,t)\)
for the coherent dynamic structure factor, also called the intermediate
scattering function \(F(q,t)\). Set \(S_c(q)=S_c(q,0)\). Use superscripts
\(s\) and \(l\) for short and long time, respectively. The convention
follows Nägele, /The Physics of Colloidal Soft Matter/ (2004), §8.2,
especially Eqs. (8.53)–(8.55) and (8.63)–(8.76).

The bulk long-time collective coefficient \(D_c^l\) is the
hydrodynamic limit of the collective diffusion kernel \(D_c(q,z)\),
at small \(q\) and \(z\) with \(q^2/z\) of order one. Equation (8.66)
gives \(S_c(q,t)\sim S_c(q)\exp(-q^2D_c^l t)\) in that limit.
The finite-window measurement in this template is denoted
\(D_{c,\mathrm{app}}(q;[t_a,t_b])\). This label describes the
estimator used here. It reserves \(D_c^l\) for a converged bulk limit.

* Choose the quantity before measuring it
| Quantity | Observable | Limit and typical repulsive-dispersion trend |
|----------+------------+----------------------------------------------|
| Long-time tagged self diffusivity \(D_s^l\) | Single-particle displacement, averaged over particles and time origins | Take the late slope of unwrapped MSD. It generally decreases with concentration in a repulsive fluid as crowding restricts tagged motion. |
| Collective diffusivity \(D_{c,\mathrm{app}}(q;[t_a,t_b])\) | Coherent equilibrium density relaxation at nonzero wave vector \(q\) | At small \(q\), and at times where the diffusive mode dominates, establish convergence toward the long-wavelength collective diffusivity. The osmotic restoring force in this repulsive system generally strengthens with concentration in the free-draining comparison. |
| Short-time collective diffusivity \(D_c^s(q)\) | Initial logarithmic decay of the normalized coherent function | For equilibrium colloids the short-time formula is \(D_0H(q)/S_c(q)\), with the mobility function and structure factor evaluated on the same ensemble. |
| EOS or sedimentation closure for collective transport | Pressure slope with a separately specified mobility factor | In the quiescent free-draining approximation the concentration derivative supplies the thermodynamic factor. Multiplying it by \(K_\mathrm{sed}\) adds a mobility model. That product and a direct time-decay measurement answer different verification questions. |

The self and collective trends need not have the same sign. Positive
repulsion suppresses long-wavelength density fluctuations and can raise
the osmotic restoring term, even when tracer motion slows down. The
mobility factor, many-body relaxation, phase, wave number and shear can
change the collective trend. In particular, the sedimentation correction
can substantially reduce or reverse a rise of the free-draining
thermodynamic factor. These statements concern an equilibrium or
local-equilibrium fluid with declared interactions and units.

mBLA consumes a collective constitutive coefficient. Under shear its
particle-transport equation calls for the relevant direction in the
diffusion tensor. The isotropic quiescent benchmark does not establish
a finite-shear \(D_{yy}\).

* Tagged-particle MSD
Use unwrapped coordinates \(\mathbf r_j^u(t)\) saved by the integrator.
Wrapped positions alias displacements across periodic boundaries.
Track the same particle ID through every observation. With no background
flow in this study, average
\[
\mathrm{MSD}(t)= \frac{1}{N}\sum_j
  \left\langle|\mathbf r_j^u(t_0+t)-\mathbf r_j^u(t_0)|^2\right\rangle_{t_0}.
\]
In three dimensions define \(W(t)=\mathrm{MSD}(t)/6\), so \(D_s^l=\lim_{t\to\infty}dW/dt\).
At finite times, estimate the slope on declared lag windows and compare
them before labeling a plateau. A tagged-particle MSD is a sum of
individual squared displacements. A squared sum of displacements would
instead give a collective center response and a different normalization.
A hydrodynamic pair-center mobility is neither of these bulk dynamic
coefficients.

* Coherent density relaxation
In a periodic cube of side \(L\), use \(\mathbf q=2\pi\mathbf n/L\) with
integer \(\mathbf n\ne0\). Track the complex amplitude
\[
 \rho_\mathbf q(t)=\sum_{j=1}^N e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r_j(t)},\qquad
 S_c(q,t)=N^{-1}\langle \rho_\mathbf q(t_0+t)\rho_\mathbf q(t_0)^*\rangle .
\]
Reciprocal-lattice modes can be computed with wrapped coordinates.
This coherent product contains \(i\ne j\) as well as \(i=j\) terms.
\(S_c(q)=S_c(q,0)\). In homogeneous equilibrium the ensemble mean of
\(\rho_\mathbf q\) is zero for \(\mathbf q\ne0\). Check stationarity and
seed variation; do not silently subtract a noisy trajectory mean or
choose an offset to enforce a desired tail.

Let \(f_c(q,t)=S_c(q,t)/S_c(q)\). The normalized logarithmic width is
\[
 W_c(q,t)=-q^{-2}\ln f_c(q,t),\qquad D_{c,\mathrm{app}}=\mathrm{slope}_{[t_a,t_b]} W_c(q,t).
\]
The width slope estimates a finite-\(q\) diffusion coefficient over the declared window.
Compare earlier and later windows, check that the fitted signal is
positive and above the noise floor, and repeat across sizes and
wavevector shells. A finite-box \(\mathbf q=0\) is a constant conserved
mode. The collective hydrodynamic limit comes from nonzero modes with
long wavelength, not from fitting that conserved mode. Agreement among
finite-\(q\) rates alone does not establish a bulk long-time value.

For free-draining overdamped BD, \(H(q)=1\) is the short-time mobility
identity and \(1/S_c(q)\) is a useful initial-rate check. A late time
fit has its own validation. The inherited mBLA thermodynamic
estimate \(B=d(\phi Z)/d\phi\) and the closure
\(K_\mathrm{sed}B\) retain their stated scope. Direct HI would require a
consistent periodic mobility and zero-mean-flow convention and its own
finite-size extrapolation.

* Reporting and verification
Retain reduced units \(D_0\) and \(\tau_\sigma=\sigma^2/D_0\), the
configuration sampling cadence, equilibration length, density shell
labels, fit windows, the effective \(q\sigma\), and separate seed curves
or fits. Always report whether the data support only finite-time and
finite-\(q\) rates or an extrapolated long-time bulk coefficient.
Use an independently computed direct time-domain correlation at
selected lags to check FFT orientation, normalization and end effects.
An ideal exponential with a known rate checks the log-slope fitting
convention. The positive signal must supply enough samples in the
fitting window. Preserve underresolved fits as missing.

For a density ladder, keep the particle model, temperature and boundary
conditions fixed. For a finite-size comparison within a concentration,
double \(N\) and \(L^3\) together, then document what changed in \(q_\min\)
and in the measured rates. Compare late windows at the final sizes and
report sampling uncertainty for the differences. Comparing the fundamental
mode of one cube with the second axial harmonic of an eightfold larger
cube holds \(q\) fixed while changing the number of particles. This checks
the box effect separately from lowering \(q_\min\).

illustrate distinct mobility observables and their comparison limits.
