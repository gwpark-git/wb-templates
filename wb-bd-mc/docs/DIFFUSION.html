Self and collective diffusion in equilibrium BD–MC

The estimators below use tagged positions and coherent density sampled on a declared observation clock. The reference model is equilibrium free-draining Brownian dynamics. Scientific validation of the full transport-analysis workflow remains pending.

Primary reference for colloidal collective diffusion

A. J. Banchio, M. Heinen, P. Holmqvist and G. Nägele, “Short- and long-time diffusion and dynamic scaling in suspensions of charged colloidal particles,” J. Chem. Phys. 148, 134902 (2018). This is the scientific reference for relating colloidal structure, density relaxation, and short- and long-time diffusion. The benchmark here uses a neutral finite-range stiff cosine interaction without HI. Its values are not a reproduction of the reference's charge-stabilized model.

The exact citation is listed as reference 69 in Nägele et al., Theory of Colloidal Suspension Structure, Dynamics, and Rheology (2021). The notation below follows the directly reviewed 2004 notes.

Dynamic structure factor and collective diffusion and relation to mBLA

Use Ss(q,t)S_s(q,t) for the self-dynamic structure factor and Sc(q,t)S_c(q,t) for the coherent dynamic structure factor, also called the intermediate scattering function F(q,t)F(q,t). Set Sc(q)=Sc(q,0)S_c(q)=S_c(q,0). Use superscripts ss and ll for short and long time, respectively. The convention follows Nägele, The Physics of Colloidal Soft Matter (2004), §8.2, especially Eqs. (8.53)–(8.55) and (8.63)–(8.76).

The bulk long-time collective coefficient DclD_c^l is the hydrodynamic limit of the collective diffusion kernel Dc(q,z)D_c(q,z), at small qq and zz with q2/zq^2/z of order one. Equation (8.66) gives Sc(q,t)∼Sc(q)exp(−q2Dclt)S_c(q,t)\sim S_c(q)\exp(-q^2D_c^l t) in that limit. The finite-window measurement in this template is denoted Dc,app(q;[ta,tb])D_{c,\mathrm{app}}(q;[t_a,t_b]). This label describes the estimator used here. It reserves DclD_c^l for a converged bulk limit.

The self and collective trends need not have the same sign. Positive repulsion suppresses long-wavelength density fluctuations and can raise the osmotic restoring term, even when tracer motion slows down. The mobility factor, many-body relaxation, phase, wave number and shear can change the collective trend. In particular, the sedimentation correction can substantially reduce or reverse a rise of the free-draining thermodynamic factor. These statements concern an equilibrium or local-equilibrium fluid with declared interactions and units.

mBLA consumes a collective constitutive coefficient. Under shear its particle-transport equation calls for the relevant direction in the diffusion tensor. The isotropic quiescent benchmark does not establish a finite-shear DyyD_{yy}.

Tagged-particle MSD

Use unwrapped coordinates 𝐫ju(t)\mathbf r_j^u(t) saved by the integrator. Wrapped positions alias displacements across periodic boundaries. Track the same particle ID through every observation. With no background flow in this study, average MSD(t)=1N∑j⟨|𝐫ju(t0+t)−𝐫ju(t0)|2⟩t0. \mathrm{MSD}(t)= \frac{1}{N}\sum_j \left\langle|\mathbf r_j^u(t_0+t)-\mathbf r_j^u(t_0)|^2\right\rangle_{t_0}. In three dimensions define W(t)=MSD(t)/6W(t)=\mathrm{MSD}(t)/6, so Dsl=limt→∞dW/dtD_s^l=\lim_{t\to\infty}dW/dt. At finite times, estimate the slope on declared lag windows and compare them before labeling a plateau. A tagged-particle MSD is a sum of individual squared displacements. A squared sum of displacements would instead give a collective center response and a different normalization. A hydrodynamic pair-center mobility is neither of these bulk dynamic coefficients.

Coherent density relaxation

In a periodic cube of side LL, use 𝐪=2π𝐧/L\mathbf q=2\pi\mathbf n/L with integer 𝐧≠0\mathbf n\ne0. Track the complex amplitude ρ𝐪(t)=∑j=1Nei𝐪⋅𝐫j(t),Sc(q,t)=N−1⟨ρ𝐪(t0+t)ρ𝐪(t0)*⟩. \rho_\mathbf q(t)=\sum_{j=1}^N e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r_j(t)},\qquad S_c(q,t)=N^{-1}\langle \rho_\mathbf q(t_0+t)\rho_\mathbf q(t_0)^*\rangle . Reciprocal-lattice modes can be computed with wrapped coordinates. This coherent product contains i≠ji\ne j as well as i=ji=j terms. Sc(q)=Sc(q,0)S_c(q)=S_c(q,0). In homogeneous equilibrium the ensemble mean of ρ𝐪\rho_\mathbf q is zero for 𝐪≠0\mathbf q\ne0. Check stationarity and seed variation; do not silently subtract a noisy trajectory mean or choose an offset to enforce a desired tail.

Let fc(q,t)=Sc(q,t)/Sc(q)f_c(q,t)=S_c(q,t)/S_c(q). The normalized logarithmic width is Wc(q,t)=−q−2lnfc(q,t),Dc,app=slope[ta,tb]Wc(q,t). W_c(q,t)=-q^{-2}\ln f_c(q,t),\qquad D_{c,\mathrm{app}}=\mathrm{slope}_{[t_a,t_b]} W_c(q,t). The width slope estimates a finite-qq diffusion coefficient over the declared window. Compare earlier and later windows, check that the fitted signal is positive and above the noise floor, and repeat across sizes and wavevector shells. A finite-box 𝐪=0\mathbf q=0 is a constant conserved mode. The collective hydrodynamic limit comes from nonzero modes with long wavelength, not from fitting that conserved mode. Agreement among finite-qq rates alone does not establish a bulk long-time value.

For free-draining overdamped BD, H(q)=1H(q)=1 is the short-time mobility identity and 1/Sc(q)1/S_c(q) is a useful initial-rate check. A late time fit has its own validation. The inherited mBLA thermodynamic estimate B=d(ϕZ)/dϕB=d(\phi Z)/d\phi and the closure KsedBK_\mathrm{sed}B retain their stated scope. Direct HI would require a consistent periodic mobility and zero-mean-flow convention and its own finite-size extrapolation.

Reporting and verification

Retain reduced units D0D_0 and τσ=σ2/D0\tau_\sigma=\sigma^2/D_0, the configuration sampling cadence, equilibration length, density shell labels, fit windows, the effective qσq\sigma, and separate seed curves or fits. Always report whether the data support only finite-time and finite-qq rates or an extrapolated long-time bulk coefficient. Use an independently computed direct time-domain correlation at selected lags to check FFT orientation, normalization and end effects. An ideal exponential with a known rate checks the log-slope fitting convention. The positive signal must supply enough samples in the fitting window. Preserve underresolved fits as missing.

For a density ladder, keep the particle model, temperature and boundary conditions fixed. For a finite-size comparison within a concentration, double NN and L3L^3 together, then document what changed in qminq_\min and in the measured rates. Compare late windows at the final sizes and report sampling uncertainty for the differences. Comparing the fundamental mode of one cube with the second axial harmonic of an eightfold larger cube holds qq fixed while changing the number of particles. This checks the box effect separately from lowering qminq_\min. The finite free-space HI pair examples illustrate distinct mobility observables and their comparison limits.
