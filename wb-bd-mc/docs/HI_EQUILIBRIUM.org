#+title: Equilibrium Brownian transport with free-space pair HI
#+date: 2026-11-25
#+language: en

* What the benchmark compares
This two-sphere test uses the same temperature, solvent, separation
interval, potential and initial conditions for three thermal dynamics:
pure BD with unit normalized mobility (\(K_\mathrm{sed}=1\)), RPY BD, and
Brownian evolution based on the deterministic Stokesian mobility with
lubrication. All use an unbounded solvent and a reflecting constraint
on the pair separation. Fluid mobility is evaluated in free space.
The pair configuration is bounded radially and free to rotate.

The Level 3 template provides the repulsive pair force, RPY mobility and
the deterministic Stokesian solver. The example builds a separate thermal
pair integrator from those mobilities. It uses neither the default
scalar BD integrator nor the full-coordinate native HI stepper for
this constrained radial experiment. It leaves both standalone solvers
available for their own problems.

Use reduced units \(\sigma=k_BT=D_0=1\), radius \(a=\sigma/2\),
\(\eta_s^*=1/(3\pi)\) and \(\tau_\sigma=\sigma^2/D_0\). The two spheres have
\(1.05\le r/\sigma\le2.50\). The template polynomial repulsion uses
\(\epsilon/k_BT=24\) and cutoff \(1.65\sigma\).
The reflecting shell retains a positive gap compatible with the SD
resistance table. Its volume sets the normalization for reported pair
stress. It is a separation-space volume and carries no bulk-volume-fraction
interpretation.

* Translational mobility and thermal dynamics
[[file:../modern/tests/benchmark_hi_equilibrium.cpp][C++ grid producer]]
calls =bdmc::hydrodynamic_mobility= and =bdmc::solve_stokesian=.
In the SD solver, opposite unit forces yield the relative diffusivities
\(D_\parallel\) and \(D_\perp\) in reduced units. Equal forces yield
the parallel and transverse center diffusivities \(D_{\mathrm{cm},\parallel}\)
and \(D_{\mathrm{cm},\perp}\) after dividing the center velocity by the
total force. Lubrication is enabled. The pure BD coefficients are 2
for relative motion and \(1/2\) for the center, in all directions.

[[file:../tools/equilibrium_hi_pair.py][Thermal pair runner]]
uses the equilibrium density \(p(r)\propto w(r)=r^2e^{-U(r)/k_BT}\).
Its radial backward generator is
\[
 Lf=w^{-1}\partial_r(wD_\parallel\,\partial_r f).
\]
The expansion gives \(D_\parallel f''+
[D'_\parallel+2D_\parallel/r-D_\parallel U'/k_BT]f'\).
The radial diffusion-gradient and geometric drifts are included through
this divergence form. Adjacent cell rates share the conductance
\(w_\mathrm{face}D_{\parallel,\mathrm{face}}/h^2\); divide by the source
center weight. Boundary faces carry no flux. Detailed balance follows
from equal forward and reverse flux at the Boltzmann weights.
The angular generator is \(D_\perp r^{-2}\Delta_\Omega\). The resulting
thermal process is the reduced two-sphere dynamics of the chosen
mobility and potential. The deterministic SD library supplies a response
matrix and stresslets. The example supplies thermal evolution separately.

* Diffusivity, viscosity and pressure
** Diffusivity
The measured equivalent of unit normalized mobility in this test is
\(H_\mathrm{pair}=2\langle 
(D_{\mathrm{cm},\parallel}+2D_{\mathrm{cm},\perp})/3\rangle/D_0\).
The factor of two normalizes to the free-draining pair reference.
The average is over the common isotropic equilibrium ensemble. Pair
mobility is a center response, whereas the relative coefficient in
\(L\) sets internal relaxation. Neither coefficient is injected as a
bulk mBLA \(D_c/D_0\). The explicit HI cases apply no empirical
\(K_\mathrm{sed}\) multiplier.

** Viscosity
Define \(q(r)=-r U'(r)\) and the smooth pair-pressure deviator
\(P^d=(q/V_\mathrm{shell})(\mathbf n\mathbf n-I/3)\).
The Green–Kubo stress contraction in isotropic equilibrium samples the
second angular harmonic. Conditional on the radial path, its
correlation factor is \(\exp[-\int_0^t6 D_\perp(r_s)/r_s^2\,ds]\).
The tensor contraction contributes \(2/3\), so the configurational
increment in simulation units is
\[
 \frac{\Delta\eta_\mathrm{conf}(t_c)}{\eta_s}
 =\frac{3\pi}{15\,V_\mathrm{shell}^*}
 \int_0^{t_c}
 \left\langle
 q^*(r_0)q^*(r_t)
 \exp[-\int_0^t6D_\perp(r_s)/r_s^2\,ds]
 \right\rangle\,dt .
\]
The spectral contraction evaluates the radial generator minus the
angular decay rate. The simulator independently samples the radial
transitions and evaluates the conditional factor along the paths.
The reported increment refers to the smooth configurational force;
the table lists both the finite \(0.4\tau_\sigma\) window and the
spectral infinite-lag limit of this bounded model.

The SD solver also returns an instantaneous stresslet response.
A symmetric traceless five-mode strain basis gives the isotropic
trace of the stresslet operator. Divide by \(2\eta_s V_\mathrm{shell}\)
to obtain a relative high-frequency increment. It is a separate
diagnostic. The RPY translational mobility provides no stresslet operator.
This experiment does not supply Brownian stresslet and stress cross
terms or a total suspension viscosity.

** Osmotic pressure and equilibrium
The common smooth-force virial contribution is
\(\langle q\rangle/(3 V_\mathrm{shell})\).
The relative shell's outer thermodynamic load is
\(k_BT e^{-U(r_\max)/k_BT}/[4\pi\int_{r_\min}^{r_\max}r^2e^{-U(r)/k_BT}dr]\).
It is the derivative of the relative configurational partition function
when increasing its outer volume at fixed inner radius. It is not the
periodic osmotic equation of state. A bulk periodic calculation would
instead evaluate the ideal particle term and all applicable interaction
virial terms in the actual box at fixed \(\phi\).

Equal weights and detailed balance predict the same equilibrium
separation law and static pressure under the three mobilities.
Different relative diffusivities and angular relaxation rates can
produce different time correlations on that same ensemble.

* Reference result and interpretation
At 144 radial cells, the following values were reproduced in the finite
pair benchmark. The pressure columns use \(k_BT/\sigma^3\).

| Model | \(H_\mathrm{pair}\) | \(\Delta\eta_\mathrm{conf}(0.4\tau_\sigma)/\eta_s\) | Smooth virial | Outer load |
|-------+---------------------+---------------------------------------------------+---------------+------------|
| Pure BD \(K_\mathrm{sed}=1\) | 1.000000e+00 | 1.409055e-03 | 3.093502e-03 | 1.822666e-02 |
| RPY BD | 1.252956e+00 | 2.037497e-03 | 3.093502e-03 | 1.822666e-02 |
| Thermal SD pair | 1.248272e+00 | 2.117308e-03 | 3.093502e-03 | 1.822666e-02 |

The SD high-frequency stresslet increment was 4.333653e-02 in its pair
normalization. In this bounded geometry, positive cross-mobility
increases center diffusion above the pure reference and relative
motion relaxes stress more slowly. The results do not determine the sign
or magnitude of a bulk sedimentation correction.

The matched pure/RPY/SD comparison is valid for the same isolated-pair
geometry, equilibrium weights and smooth-force definitions. Within that
comparison, \(H_\mathrm{pair}\) and the finite configurational integral
are common observables. The SD stresslet is a model-specific component.
A comparison with the periodic mBLA coupling study additionally requires
a periodic mobility with a specified mean-flow convention, a common
particle model and density, and explicit structure and stress definitions.
For a finite-wavevector short-time measurement one would compare the
hydrodynamic function \(H(q)\) and structure factor \(S(q)\) on the
same equilibrium states, before assessing a bulk \(q\to0\) limit.
The earlier pure-BD \(K_\mathrm{sed}=1\) thermodynamic factor is
\(\partial_\phi(\phi Z)\). It is a separate quantity from \(H_\mathrm{pair}\).

For the corresponding periodic observables, see [[file:DIFFUSION.org][self and collective diffusion]].

* Reproduce the benchmark
Requires C++17, CMake and GSL for the native grid, and Python with NumPy
and SciPy for the thermal calculation. Run from the template directory.

#+begin_src sh
work="$(mktemp -d)"
cmake -S modern -B "$work/build" -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release \
  -DBDMC_BUILD_PYTHON=OFF -DBDMC_BUILD_BENCHMARKS=ON -DBDMC_BUILD_HYDRODYNAMICS=ON
cmake --build "$work/build" --parallel 2 --target bdmc_hi_equilibrium_grid
mkdir "$work/grids"
for cells in 72 144 288; do
  "$work/build/bdmc_hi_equilibrium_grid" "$cells" > "$work/grids/mobility-$cells.csv"
done
python3 tools/equilibrium_hi_pair.py --grids "$work/grids" --output "$work/results"
#+end_src

Output tables record mobility/transport, sampled equilibrium endpoints
from two different starts, the running Green–Kubo integral, and sampled
finite integrals with Monte Carlo standard error. Floating values use
17 significant digits in scientific notation. A grid refinement,
analytical RPY pair, isolated SD stresslet, far-separation drag and
paired thermal-equilibrium checks are included in this reference workflow.
The 144-cell stress test uses 4,096 paths for each of two seeds.

* Fixed-density finite-size test
The pair result supplies a check of the thermal model. To test size
sensitivity, a second example samples equilibrium configurations in a cube
with reflecting center boundaries. It matches \(\phi=0.10\),
\(N=8,16,32,64\) and \(L/\sigma=(N\pi/(6\phi))^{1/3}\).
The pair energy and minimum separation are the same as above.
Each doubling in \(N\) scales the side by \(2^{1/3}\).
The mechanical cube admits particle centers. The mobility solver still
uses unbounded solvent, without periodic images or fluid walls.

[[file:../modern/tests/benchmark_hi_size.cpp][C++ finite-size benchmark]]
equilibrates each size with single-particle Metropolis moves. Use two
seeds, discard 2,000 sweeps, and then record six blocks of 160 sweeps
per seed. Compute mobility and SD stresslets on the same last configuration
of each block. The Markov chain samples equilibrium. Its sweep number
does not set a Brownian time. This design compares the equal-time
mobility and the instantaneous SD stresslet. Only the separate pair
experiment measures a thermal stress correlation.

Apply equal force to each sphere in three Cartesian directions and compute
\[
 H_0^\mathrm{open}
 =\frac{1}{3N\mu_0}\left\langle\sum_{ij}\operatorname{tr}M_{ij}\right\rangle .
\]
The free-draining identity gives one. Evaluate the SD stresslet in a
symmetric \(xy\) affine strain and normalize by \(2\eta_s V E:E\).
The reported SD quantity represents this resolved stresslet contribution.
The benchmark provides no equivalent RPY stresslet.

For pressure sample each fourth sweep of every production block.
Use virtual compression \(V'=Ve^{-\epsilon}\) with
\(\epsilon=0.002,0.004\). The compression estimator is
\[
 \Pi^* =
 \frac{N\epsilon-\ln\langle e^{-\Delta U/k_BT}
       \mathbf 1_\mathrm{allowed}\rangle}{(V-V')/\sigma^3}.
\]
Particle-center distances scale by \(e^{-\epsilon/3}\); a compressed
configuration with a disallowed gap contributes zero weight.
The \(N\epsilon\) term comes from the coordinate Jacobian.
Use the smaller compression step for the primary number and retain
both to show finite perturbation sensitivity. This is the mechanical
confinement pressure of the chosen cube. It is shared by all three
mobility models on the same samples.

| \(N\) | \(L/\sigma\) | Pure BD \(H_0\) | RPY \(H_0^\mathrm{open}\) | SD \(H_0^\mathrm{open}\) | SD stresslet increment | \(\Pi^*,\epsilon=0.002\) |
|-------+--------------+-------------------+---------------------------+--------------------------+------------------------+-------------------------|
| 8 | 3.472931 | 1.000000 | 2.417993 | 2.387130 | 2.541751e-01 | 2.967094e-01 |
| 16 | 4.375619 | 1.000000 | 3.647401 | 3.576062 | 2.519200e-01 | 3.202489e-01 |
| 32 | 5.512935 | 1.000000 | 5.508111 | 5.376758 | 2.530998e-01 | 3.652777e-01 |
| 64 | 6.945863 | 1.000000 | 8.645013 | 8.347808 | 2.561289e-01 | 3.833725e-01 |

The size trend in open-fluid mobility is consistent with summing the
long-range \(1/r\) coupling under a net common force. It does not
converge to bulk \(K_\mathrm{sed}\) in this test. The SD instantaneous
stresslet varies much less over the tested sizes. The pressure changes
with the finite cube size, even on the shared equilibrium ensemble.
Two seeds and one density bound the interpretation. Compact plots can
show the seed-to-seed range and both compression steps, without treating
them as a confidence interval or extrapolating thermodynamic limits.

Reproduce from the same configured template build.

#+begin_src sh
cmake --build "$work/build" --parallel 2 --target bdmc_hi_size
mkdir "$work/sizes"
for seed in 51367 93481; do
  for n in 8 16 32 64; do
    "$work/build/bdmc_hi_size" "$n" "$seed" > "$work/sizes/N$n-seed$seed.csv"
  done
done
#+end_src
