#+title: Periodic hard-sphere Monte Carlo and osmotic pressure
#+date: 2026-11-25
#+language: en

* Scientific scope
The standalone sampler produces equilibrium configurations of monodisperse
hard spheres in a periodic cubic box at fixed particle count and volume. The
temperature enters the pressure normalization. The hard-core measure is uniform
on nonoverlapping configurations. The sampler attaches no physical time to its
updates. The soft reference experiment instead uses LAMMPS explicit overdamped
Brownian dynamics with a finite cosine potential, Brownian time, and pair-force
pressure. Both engines report EOS observables at the same nominal diameter,
number density and temperature scale.

* Physical and mathematical update
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: mc-update
:END:
Use the particle diameter \(\sigma\) as the length unit. In a cube of side \(L\),
the relative periodic displacement is the componentwise minimum image
\(\Delta r_{ij,\alpha} \mapsto \Delta r_{ij,\alpha} -
L\,\mathrm{round}(\Delta r_{ij,\alpha}/L)\).
The set \(\Omega\) contains configurations for which every minimum-image
distance is at least one. The dimensionless potential is zero on \(\Omega\)
and \(+\infty\) outside it.

One trial proceeds in this order.

1. Select a particle label uniformly from \(1,\ldots,N\), with replacement
   across trials. Draw each of three displacement components independently
   and uniformly in \([-d,d]\). The native code uses the retained PCG32
   generator with unbiased bounded label selection.
2. Propose \(\mathbf r_i'=(\mathbf r_i+\boldsymbol{\delta})\bmod L\).
   Inspect the 27 linked cells surrounding the *target* cell. Cell width is
   at least 1.05 diameters, so these cells cover all possible overlaps and the
   largest measured contact shell. The initialization also checks every pair
   independently of the cell list.
3. Accept iff \(r_{ij}'{}^2\geq 1\) for every other particle. On acceptance
   update the wrapped coordinate, displacement bookkeeping and cell links.
   On rejection retain the previous particle state. The random stream has
   consumed the proposal in either outcome. The implementation rejects
   strictly below one in floating-point geometry.

The displacement law is symmetric on the torus, so the proposal density
satisfies \(q(\mathbf R,\mathbf R')=q(\mathbf R',\mathbf R)\). For two allowed
configurations the Metropolis ratio is one, since both have the same
Boltzmann weight. A proposed overlapping configuration has zero weight and
is rejected, contributing a self-transition. This establishes detailed
balance for the target configurational measure in the mathematical rule.
Mixing and sampling error remain finite-run questions. One sweep counts
\(N\) such trials. The step \(d\) controls acceptance and mixing. The optional
unwrapped displacement is bookkeeping for the Markov chain. Physical Brownian
diffusion, shear relaxation and viscosity require a dynamics model and a
physical clock.

* Soft Brownian model and physical time
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: brownian-time
:END:
The original LAMMPS study used the cosine interaction to vary repulsion at
fixed nominal diameter and density with one parameter. The current comparison
retains that law for direct replay. At fixed cutoff diameter \(\sigma\),
\(U(r)=A[1+\cos(\pi r/\sigma)]\) for \(0\le r<\sigma\) and zero for
\(r\ge\sigma\). The outward radial force is
\((\pi A/\sigma)\sin(\pi r/\sigma)\) inside the cutoff. Both potential and
force reach zero continuously there. The center barrier is \(2A\).
Increasing \(A/k_BT\) from 10 to 100 raises the energy cost of penetration
while preserving the shape and nominal \(\phi=\pi N\sigma^3/(6V)\).
These amplitudes are finite. Thermal penetration and the choice of timestep
affect the comparison to excluded-volume spheres. The template's polynomial
BD is a distinct interaction. [[https://github.com/lammps/lammps/blob/611ca3b7f8525ba802373b04f9e3d632d515f7e3/doc/src/pair_soft.rst][LAMMPS soft potential, checked edition]]

The separate physical dynamics is the overdamped Itô equation with
configuration-independent single-particle mobility
\(\mu_0=\gamma_t^{-1}\) and bare diffusivity \(D_0=k_BT\mu_0\).
LAMMPS uses the explicit old-state update
\(\Delta\mathbf r_i=\mu_0\mathbf F_i\Delta t+
\sqrt{2D_0\Delta t}\,\mathbf G_i\), with independent unit normal components
in \(\mathbf G_i\). This makes \(2D_0\Delta t\) the free diffusion variance
per coordinate. The diameter-based Brownian time is
\(\tau_\sigma=\sigma^2/D_0\), so a reduced time \(t^*=n\Delta t^*\) maps
to \(t=t^*\tau_\sigma\). At radius \(a=\sigma/2\), the radius-based convention
is \(\tau_a=a^2/D_0=\tau_\sigma/4\). For an isolated sphere with hydrodynamic
diameter \(\sigma\), Stokes–Einstein gives
\(D_0=k_BT/(3\pi\eta_s\sigma)\). The soft simulation sets
\(\sigma=k_BT=\gamma_t=D_0=1\). [[https://github.com/lammps/lammps/blob/611ca3b7f8525ba802373b04f9e3d632d515f7e3/doc/src/fix_brownian.rst][LAMMPS Brownian update, checked edition]]

In the hard MC run, one sweep counts \(N\) trials of the configurational
chain. Changing the trial width changes that chain's displacement per sweep
while keeping its equilibrium target. A dynamics-based hard-sphere transport
calculation would need its own evolution rule, collision treatment and time
validation. Even the free-particle trial variance gives only a dilute
reference for a dynamical mapping. The present MC trajectories establish
equilibrium quantities such as pressure and contact structure.

* Pressure observables and method references
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: virtual-compression
:END:
With \(\rho=6\phi/\pi\) in reduced units, the soft osmotic pressure is the
ideal contribution \(\rho\) plus the pair virial divided by \(3V\).
The instantaneous virial is \(\sum_{i<j}\mathbf r_{ij}\cdot\mathbf F_{ij}\),
with periodic minimum-image vectors and each unordered pair counted once.
The LAMMPS observable uses =compute pressure NULL pair=. The ideal term
uses \(k_BT\) at the imposed bath temperature. The overdamped displacement
increment serves the position update. [[https://github.com/lammps/lammps/blob/611ca3b7f8525ba802373b04f9e3d632d515f7e3/doc/src/compute_pressure.rst][LAMMPS pair pressure, checked edition]]

For hard-sphere pressure, Allen and Tildesley give an explicit precedent in
the companion NVT program for /Computer Simulation of Liquids/, 2nd ed.
(Oxford University Press, 2017). In =calc_variables= the program shrinks the
box length from \(L\) to \(L/(1+\epsilon)\), counts the overlapping pairs
\(C_\epsilon\), and uses \(C_\epsilon/(3\epsilon)\) as the virial estimator.
This estimates the small-compression limit using a pair count. Our study
instead calculates the probability that the entire configuration survives
the compression and uses a finite logarithmic difference. The prior study
recorded the same pressure identity and fixed \(x\) before execution.
[[https://github.com/Allen-Tildesley/examples/blob/master/python_examples/mc_nvt_hs.py][Allen–Tildesley NVT example]]

For the logarithmic difference choose \(u=\ln(V/V')\) and \(x=Nu\).
Thus \(V'=Ve^{-u}\) specifies a one-sided change of volume, and
\(u=3\ln(1+\epsilon)\) for the box perturbation above. Write the hard-sphere
configurational integral \(Q(V)\) in fractional coordinates at fixed \(N\),
\(T\) and \(\sigma\). The affine coordinate transformation to the smaller box
has Jacobian \((V'/V)^N=e^{-Nu}\). The hard-core Boltzmann weight on the
original equilibrium configurations becomes the survival indicator. Scaling
lengths by \(e^{-u/3}\) leaves the configuration allowed iff
\(r_\min/\sigma\ge e^{u/3}\). Taking the equilibrium mean \(p_u\) of that
indicator yields
\[
\frac{Q(V')}{Q(V)}=e^{-Nu}p_u,\qquad
Z_x=-\frac{\ln Q(V')-\ln Q(V)}{Nu}=1-\frac{\ln p_u}{x}.
\]
The ideal term one is the coordinate Jacobian. With
\(\Pi=k_BT\,\partial_V\ln Q\), integration gives
\(Z_x=u^{-1}\int_0^u Z(\phi e^s)\,ds\). The finite \(x\) result therefore
averages the compressibility factor over the compressed density interval.
The limit \(u\to0^+\) gives the differential pressure at the original density.
At \(x=0.25,N=216\), the volume reduction is about \(0.116\%\).
Keep the prespecified \(x=0.25\) result and use the other compressions to
assess sensitivity. Zero observed survival and queries beyond the recorded
minimum-distance cap are explicit failures.

An alternate estimator from the same configurations uses exact shell volume.
For mean unordered pair count \(C_w\) within \(1\leq r<1+w\), the canonical
finite-\(N\) normalization gives \(Z_w=1+C_w/[N((1+w)^3-1)]\).
It approaches the contact-route pressure for small \(w\) with sufficient
sampling. A finite shell is an average, with its own bandwidth sensitivity.
Agreement with the volume route should be checked as a separate statistic.
Carnahan–Starling is an external equilibrium EOS comparator. A finite
soft repulsion may give lower pressure than the nominal-diameter hard-sphere
reference without indicating a software error.
[[https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1672048][Carnahan and Starling, J. Chem. Phys. 51, 635–636 (1969)]]

* Transport observables and their interpretation
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: transport-observables
:END:
The equilibrium soft BD trajectories retain periodic image counters.
Reconstruct \(\mathbf r_i^\mathrm{unwrap}(t)=\mathbf r_i(t)+L\mathbf I_i(t)\).
Average \(\lvert\mathbf r_i^\mathrm{unwrap}(t+\Delta t)-
\mathbf r_i^\mathrm{unwrap}(t)\rvert^2\) over particles and available origins.
The three-dimensional long-time Einstein relation uses one sixth of the
MSD slope. A finite fitted window estimates tagged-particle
\(D_s^\mathrm{eff}/D_0\). Compare slopes across windows before making an
asymptotic claim. Long-wavelength collective diffusion is a separate property.
See [[file:DIFFUSION.org][self and collective diffusion]] for the coherent density estimator,
wavevector limits and the expected concentration trends.

For equilibrium constant-mobility BD without interparticle HI, an EOS-based
thermodynamic estimate uses
\(D_c/D_0=(k_BT)^{-1}\partial\Pi/\partial\rho=\partial_\phi[\phi Z(\phi)]\)
at fixed potential and temperature. An interval secant estimates the average
thermodynamic factor over that interval. A dilute endpoint may use
\(\lim_{\phi\to0} Z=1\). The estimate does not measure the time decay of
collective density modes or a direction-dependent diffusivity under shear.

For viscosity use the symmetric configurational pair-pressure tensor in
units \(k_BT/\sigma^3\), with only the pair forces of the soft model. With
mean-subtracted deviatoric tensor \(\delta\boldsymbol{\Pi}^d\), the isotropic
equilibrium Green–Kubo integrand is
\(G_\mathrm{conf}(t)=(V/k_BT)\langle
\delta\boldsymbol{\Pi}^d(0):\delta\boldsymbol{\Pi}^d(t)\rangle/10\).
Integrate to a declared lag to obtain the finite configurational
contribution. Its unit is \(k_BT\tau_\sigma/\sigma^3\). Under the
Stokes–Einstein diameter convention, adding the solvent once gives the
partial ratio \(\eta_\mathrm{partial}/\eta_s=1+3\pi\eta_\mathrm{conf}^*\).
This includes the modeled configurational relaxation and the chosen
solvent term. Hydrodynamic stresslets, lubrication and other missing stress
terms require a separate model. Lag-window and replica sensitivity determine
the strength of any viscosity inference.

* Standalone entrypoint and verification
#+begin_src sh
cmake -S modern -B /tmp/bdmc-equilibrium -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release \
  -DBDMC_BUILD_PYTHON=OFF -DBDMC_BUILD_HYDRODYNAMICS=OFF \
  -DBDMC_BUILD_HARD_SPHERE_MC=ON
cmake --build /tmp/bdmc-equilibrium --parallel 2
ctest --test-dir /tmp/bdmc-equilibrium --output-on-failure
/tmp/bdmc-equilibrium/equilibrium/bdmc_hard_sphere_mc \
  0.15 6 0.15 91371 11 10000 100000 5 > hard_phi015.csv
#+end_src

The positional inputs are volume fraction, particles per side of a simple
cubic start, displacement half-width, PCG seed and stream, discarded sweeps,
production sweeps and sample stride. The optional final argument =fcc=
starts from shuffled FCC sites with vacancies at the same particle count
and volume. Its initialization RNG is separate from the MC proposal
stream. The default is the earlier simple-cubic start. CSV records sweep, capped minimum
separation squared, cumulative unordered counts for widths 0.0025 to 0.04,
unwrapped MC displacement and acceptance. Clear physical interpretation and
immutable scalar data make the observable usable by a separate process.
The analysis example reads selected hard-MC CSV and LAMMPS run tables with
explicit plans and archived comparators, and writes a common table and a
PDF/PNG pair. Raw study data are separate from the template source.

The native tests check exact contact, periodic overlap, shell counting,
ideal moves and equality of linked-cell and brute-force seeded trajectories.
The EOS case uses \(N=216\), \(\phi=0.05,0.15,0.30\), four MC replicas per
condition and \(\sigma=1\). Soft BD uses \(A/k_BT=10,100\), three replicas
per condition, time step \(10^{-5}\), two Brownian times discarded and eight
sampled. Keep engine, model, seed, clock, pressure estimator and uncertainty
with every reused result. These equilibrium outputs can qualify pressure
or structure workflows. Cross-scale transport qualification remains separate.

* Level 3 exchange contract and acceptance scope
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: level-3-acceptance
:END:
The reusable slice is equilibrium pressure and near-contact structure for
monodisperse spheres in a quiescent periodic cube. The native producer owns
its sampler state and PCG stream. An independent Python process can consume
its CSV using NumPy and the functions in =bdmc.equilibrium=. The producer
does not need Python, LAMMPS, mBLA or a wb plugin at runtime.

| Boundary | Contract |
|----------+----------|
| Hard-sphere observations | Nine named columns in the order emitted by the CLI. =sweep= counts trial batches. =min_r2_capped= is the minimum squared separation in diameter units, capped at \(1.05^2\). The five cumulative shell columns count unordered pairs. =mc_msd= is displacement in squared diameter units on the trial clock. =acceptance= is the fraction of accepted proposals in the latest output block. |
| Pressure exchange | \(Z=\Pi/(\rho k_BT)\), a dimensionless equilibrium osmotic pressure, paired with nominal \(\phi\), \(N\), model identity and estimator. Hard MC uses a compression survival secant or a finite contact shell; LAMMPS soft BD uses the pair virial with the ideal term. |
| State and reproducibility | Keep temperature/length conventions, potential and amplitude or hard exclusion, seed and stream, box and boundary conditions, equilibration, sampling cadence, fit/estimator windows, uncertainty and source/data edition alongside values. |
| Consumer and domain | The comparison consumes pressure at sampled concentrations. Conversion to Pa needs the receiving model's \(k_BT\) and number density. A table, interpolation and applicability checks belong to the receiving workflow. Dynamic coefficients or a new scale mapping require independent qualification. |

The native test checks contact, periodic overlap, shell counts and seeded
cell/brute-force agreement. The retained numerical replay checks the imported
MC behavior against the original recorded observations. Independent physical
evidence comes from the Carnahan–Starling comparison and a separate
position-based check of the soft-BD virial. The comparable observable is
pressure at a matched nominal state; the soft and hard interaction laws carry
different model identities.

For a compact detached producer–consumer check, use the build above from the
copied package root with NumPy available. Save the CSV in a fresh directory.

#+begin_src sh
sample_dir="$(mktemp -d)"
/tmp/bdmc-equilibrium/equilibrium/bdmc_hard_sphere_mc \
  0.15 3 0.15 91371 11 1000 4000 5 > "$sample_dir/hard_smoke.csv"
PYTHONPATH=python python3 - "$sample_dir/hard_smoke.csv" <<'PY'
import sys

import numpy as np
from bdmc.equilibrium import hard_sphere_survival, virtual_compression_z

data = np.genfromtxt(sys.argv[1], delimiter=",", names=True)
np.testing.assert_array_equal(data["sweep"], np.arange(1005, 5001, 5))
for compression in (0.25, 0.50):
    survives = hard_sphere_survival(data["min_r2_capped"], particles=27,
                                    compression=compression)
    print(compression, virtual_compression_z(survives.mean(), compression))
PY
#+end_src

Changing compression exercises the same recorded geometry with a different
estimator setting. A compression beyond the distance cap is rejected. Short
fixed-seed runs demonstrate data exchange; use the longer replicated study
for EOS comparisons. The active-shear transient-network modernization
remains a separate workflow with an open Level 2 acceptance step.
